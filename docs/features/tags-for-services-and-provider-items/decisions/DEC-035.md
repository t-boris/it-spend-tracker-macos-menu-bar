## Context

AC-008 and plan I-4 ask to sum targets only when their costs are provably disjoint, and leave the evidence to the implementation ("Define the evidence used to establish disjointness"). Data available: per-key month cost, breakdown rows per month, dimension and label (rows of one dimension are merged by label), Neon metric rows per project, and the service's own monthly spend.

## Alternatives

1. Treat keys and breakdown items as disjoint unless shown to overlap (risks double counting)
2. Never sum more than one target per service (loses the plain key-only totals that REQ-013 of the main feature already gives)

## Decision

Within one tracked service and one tag: (1) a tagged service contributes its monthly spend once and covers every other tagged target of that service; (2) otherwise tagged targets are summed only when all of those with a known cost belong to one disjoint class: all keys (each key's cost is the provider's attribution to that key, as REQ-013 of the main feature already sums them), or all breakdown items of the same dimension (distinct labels partition that dimension's month), or all Neon projects; (3) a mix of classes (a key with a breakdown item, two different dimensions, a project row with a key) is not provably disjoint, so the service's part is unresolved. Targets of different services are always disjoint. Targets with an unavailable cost are listed but take no part in the check.

## Reason

These are the only disjointness facts the stored data proves; anything else (for example a key against a project row) may be the same dollars seen from two angles, and the overview prefers an unavailable total to a misleading one.

## Consequences

A tag holding both a key and a project row of one service has an unavailable total (DEC-034) until one of them is untagged or the service itself is tagged. Marked proposed: it is the AI's reading of what the plan leaves to the implementation, for the owner to accept.
